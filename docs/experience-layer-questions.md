### Six persistent gaps — domestic prospective, undergraduate [Dovetail · 2024–2026]

The same six themes recur across both the 2024 and 2025–26 VOC eras, filtered to domestic + undergraduate prospective:

1. **Fees & cost clarity**
2. **Entry requirements & admissions** — ATAR / entry scores, prerequisites and selection criteria visible per course, in one place.
3. **Career outcomes** — what jobs / careers a degree leads to; careers mapped to specialisations.
4. **Course structure & subject detail** — concrete subjects, majors/minors, electives and learning outcomes, not generic overviews.
5. **Navigation, findability & page design** — hard to find specific info; dated visual design; colour, hierarchy, larger fonts.
6. **Intake & application dates** — *moderate evidence.* When the course starts and when applications open / close.

*Caveat on ranking:* Dovetail labels fees "strongest, most persistent" across the whole folder, but in this persona's cut (Aug 2025–26, domestic current high-school) fees is 3.1% of verbatims — behind visual design, ATAR and content depth. The folder-wide ranking likely reflects VE and concession fees from broader segments, so it is not treated as the top theme for this persona.

*Counter-evidence / balance:* "a meaningful minority praise the pages as clear and easy to navigate," and the newer pathway diagrams "drew unprompted praise from a careers practitioner." The themes are improvement requests layered on a generally positive base, not signs of a failing experience.

## Appendix — MIP 2026 Domestic Demand Outlook (as provided, verbatim)

From MIP 2026 Domestic Demand Outlook:

Five year projections for domestic commencements is for less than 1% annual growth between 2027 and 2031.
- Higher Education: full market growth potential unlikely to be realised by RMIT.
- RMIT HE limited either by funding agreements, or lower competitive performance vs other providers.
- UG: supply to be determined via Mission based compacts.
- Sub-degrees: the market at diploma level is becoming increasingly competitive with offers available to students from other providers. Demand may, therefore, shift away from associate degree programs.
- PG: there is likely to be some market growth, but RMIT's share is likely to continue to decline without major interventions.
- TAFE: market is slowing but still good potential pipeline growth for RMIT
- RMIT should be able to continue planned growth, albeit at potentially slower rates than the current five-year forecast.

Victoria's domestic tertiary market is potentially entering a period of higher volatility (into 2027) followed by structural realignment (2027–2031).
- Short term (2026–27):
  - Policy reform, elections, cost-of-living pressure and key workforce shortages likely to continue to dominate demand and student decision-making.
  - Growing societal risk from potential impacts to current and prospective student behaviour:
    - Inflation continuing to grow. RBA raised cash rates on 17 March contracting discretionary household spend.
    - Middle-East situation – any requirement for petrol rationing could impact travel to campus
    - At time of writing, any potential impact(s) from the above are unknown.
- Medium term (2028–32):
  - System change (ATEC, managed growth funding), plus the consistent need for upskilling and training could start to change the long term direction of participation in education.

Potential Implications:
- University demand will continue to become more price and employability-driven.
- TAFE demand should remain structurally strong and politically protected.
- Pathways, applied degrees and microcredentials becoming essential core growth engines.
  - Skills-based, stackable product development.
  - New segment(s) for mature learners: career changers, refreshers, re-skillers
  - Easing access for mature leaners (cost, delivery & RPL) and equity cohorts

2026–27 (Next 1–2 years)
1. Political volatility: the Victorian election & Free TAFE (Nov 2026)
Election cycle and associated media coverage may drive policy-driven demand swings, increasing unpredictability. Education funding is becoming a major political weapon, especially amid cost-of-living stress. It is expected that educational affordability, debt levels, Free TAFE, HE funding and student support will continue to be weaponised politically.
Victoria has embedded Free TAFE as a core electoral platform. However, with a large state cash deficit, further TAFE defunding could arise during 2027/28. VET demand should remain structurally strong, but if "cuts" are noted publicly, it signals higher out-of-pocket costs for price-sensitive cohorts, such as lower-income and mature-age learners. This might create some "wait and see" behaviour later in 2026 for 2027 study decisions.
2. Workforce shortages
Strong continued pull into health, teaching, trades, energy, construction and digital, boosting TAFE and applied HE.
- Continued demand uplift for nursing, teaching, trades with strong pipeline for skills-first learners
3. Cost-of-living pressure
Financial pressure is now a much more dominant behavioural driver for domestic students, which is shaping: participation, study choice, mode of study and load intensity.
- All of which is likely to drive appeal of TAFE, part-time, blended and short credentials; whilst softening demand for long, "high-debt" degrees.
4. Demographic pipelines
The year 12 school-leaver cohort in Victoria is projected to grow minimally in the next few years.
Growth in older potential learners (25-59 yrs) will also slow after 2026, however may be short-term opportunities in Masters' programs amongst students in their twenties, as projections suggest 2027-2029 will have the strongest demographic growth in the next five years (~2.5% p.a.).
5. Policy & funding reform (Universities Accord)
2026 transition + 2027 Managed Growth Funding steer demand (and focus) toward equity cohorts, skills-aligned programs and pathways.
Net effects (2026–27):
- Demand for TAFE and applied HE learning should stabilse and grow rather more slowly, but consistently.
- HE may become more volatile, but will be increasingly price-sensitive and employment-driven.
- Program portfolio strategy, availability of evening/online/hybrid delivery and good RPL will increasingly become more important demand levers.

2028-2031 (3–5 years)
1. Ongoing political exposure
Education embedded in cost-of-living politics → persistent funding and demand volatility. Increased risk exposure from domestic exposure as well as international markets.
2. Structural system redesign
From demand-led to supply driven model, with needs-based funding shifting focus to widening access, including pathways, equity cohorts and applied education.
3. Long-term skills transition
Energy, infrastructure, health and care needs will continue to drive multi-decadal training demand, structurally benefiting TAFE. Especially across clean energy, construction, health/ aged care, teaching, plus specific areas of IT demand (e.g. cyber).
4. Demographics
Moderate youth growth will limit UG volume growth; but there should be continued growth in upskilling and mid-career learning. This requires growth to come from new learner segments, not school-leavers and enable easy access to education for these cohorts.
5. Student mindset shift
ROI-focused decisions → preference for short, stackable, employment-linked credentials. Outcomes, not degrees are the focus. Concern around technology, energy transition and AI impacting future employment also likely to continue to shift discipline choice. Prospective students of all ages will continuing to seek the most cost and time efficient way to reach desired career outcomes.
6. Job market and Employer expectations
Forecasts suggest job markets will become less buoyant, potentially offering more upskilling opportunity. However, if employer perceptions of worker shortages and inconsistent quality of completed students, this may exacerbate a reduced reliance on formal qualifications in favour of in-house and shorter skills-based learning (weakening long-run demand).
Net effects (post-2027):
- Consistent and persistent demand shift toward skills, pathways, microcredentials and shorter, but regular upskilling/ learning.
